## C2ST Diagnostic Investigation and Sensitivity Analysis

The prespecified Logistic C2ST showed weak held-out discrimination (AUC 0.51771) but a small permutation p-value (0.006; Holm-adjusted 0.012 across the original two classifiers). Only two of 499 null AUCs reached the observed value. Statistical significance therefore coexisted with limited predictive separation; it did not establish a practically important imbalance or randomization failure.

We investigated coefficient directions, scaling sensitivity, and feature tails using the same 20,000 balanced diagnostic observations and fixed 14,000/6,000 training/holdout split. After the original diagnostic cache was found missing, an authorized full pipeline rerun regenerated it. Ordered sample identifiers matched the original hash, and the baseline AUC and all 499 permutation AUCs were reproduced. The investigation then read only this cache. Logistic settings remained fixed, including L2 regularization, C=1, the lbfgs solver, max_iter=500, and seed=42.

The largest standardized coefficients belonged to f8 (−0.07176) and f1 (+0.04953), both aligned with their full-source SMDs. Although f3 and f6 also had negative coefficients, their magnitudes were relatively small; f5 had a positive coefficient despite its negative SMD. Nine of twelve coefficient directions aligned with full-source SMDs. This is consistent with multiple small differences contributing to linear distinguishability, but does not identify individual contributions. Correlations, including f5/f7 (r=−0.756) and f8/f9 (r=−0.742), limit marginal interpretations of conditional coefficients. Coefficient–SMD products are directional diagnostics, not causal effects or an AUC decomposition.

Replacing StandardScaler with RobustScaler changed AUC by only −0.000445 to 0.51727. All coefficient signs remained unchanged. Using the same 499 train/holdout label permutations and full refits gave an exploratory p-value of 0.010. This result was kept separate from the original Holm family. Eight training features had zero IQR, for which the fitted RobustScaler used a scale of one. Scaling also changes the effective penalty in original feature units, so the comparison does not isolate outlier influence alone.

Tail summaries revealed mass points, skewness, and descriptive arm differences. For f6, 1.23% of treatment versus 0.75% of control observations fell below the pooled first percentile; for f1, 0.46% versus 0.20% exceeded the pooled 99th percentile. These observations motivated one fixed 1%/99% winsorization sensitivity check. Thresholds were estimated from pooled training features and applied identically to both arms and the holdout, followed by the original StandardScaler and Logistic model. AUC increased slightly to 0.51928. No winsorization p-value was reported because this check was selected after inspecting arm-specific tails.

The weak signal persisted under both preprocessing alternatives. These findings do not support attributing the original result solely to StandardScaler or a few extreme observations. They also do not establish the source of the distributional differences, validate permutation exchangeability under unknown experimental structure, or quantify bias in treatment-effect or policy-value estimates. The interpretation of randomization quality remains cautious: weak observable assignment predictability warrants disclosure and investigation, but is not proof that the original experiment failed. No main-analysis model, selection, or inferential result was replaced.
